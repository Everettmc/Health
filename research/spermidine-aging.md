# Spermidine and Aging: An Evidence Review

*Same method and grading as the [mitochondrial-health review](mitochondrial-health-interventions.md). Last updated 2026-11-02.*

**Bottom line:**

- **The mechanism is real, and so are the mouse and fly data.** Spermidine induces autophagy and extends lifespan in yeast, worms, flies and mice [[1]][s1] [[2]][s2].
- **In humans, the only well-powered, long trial was null.** SmartAge gave 0.9 mg/day for 12 months to 100 older adults. Memory, the primary outcome, did not change, and neither did the secondary biomarkers [[6]][s6].
- **The positive human results so far are small pilots or exploratory secondary analyses**, mostly from groups linked to the commercial product [[5]][s5] [[7]][s7] [[8]][s8] [[20]][s20].
- **Commercial doses look too low to matter.** Typical supplements deliver about 1 mg/day. A normal diet already supplies roughly 8–12 mg/day [[14]][s14] [[18]][s18]. Even 15 mg/day did **not raise blood spermidine** [[10]][s10], and 40 mg/day barely changed circulating polyamines [[11]][s11].
- **The longevity link in humans comes from diet questionnaires.** These observational studies don't agree, and food spermidine travels with whole grains, legumes and vegetables [[3]][s3] [[15]][s15] [[16]][s16] [[17]][s17].
- **There is a plausible, unresolved cancer concern.** Polyamines feed cell growth, and *lowering* them prevents colorectal adenomas [[13]][s13] [[21]][s21].

**Verdict:**
- **Evidence grade for "slows aging in humans": C.**
- **Relative impact: about 3/100**, on the same scale as the mitochondrial review. That puts it level with PQQ, far below exercise.
- **Don't buy it for longevity.** The most informative pending result is the POLYCAD trial (24 mg/day, 187 cardiac patients), which has not yet been published [[19]][s19].

## 1. What spermidine is and why people take it

Spermidine is a polyamine (the others are putrescine and spermine). Every cell makes it, and it also comes from food and gut bacteria. The anti-aging case rests on three linked claims.

| Claim | Evidence | Species | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spermidine induces **autophagy**, and autophagy is required for its lifespan effect | Lifespan extended in yeast, flies and worms, and in human immune cells in culture. The effect disappeared when autophagy was blocked [[1]][s1]. | Yeast, worms, flies, human cells 🐭 | D |
| Oral spermidine **extends mouse lifespan and protects the heart** | Less cardiac hypertrophy, better diastolic function, and more cardiac mitophagy and mitochondrial respiration in old mice. **No cardioprotection without the autophagy gene Atg5** [[2]][s2]. | Mice and rats 🐭 | D |
| Spermidine levels **fall with age**, and restoring them **rejuvenates immune cells** | Spermidine maintains autophagy through **eIF5A hypusination and TFEB** (a translation factor and a master autophagy switch). Adding spermidine restored function in T cells and B cells taken from older human donors [[22]][s22] [[23]][s23]. It also restored memory T-cell responses in old mice [[24]][s24]. | Human cells (outside the body); mice | C (mechanistic human) |
| **Fasting works partly through spermidine** | Fasting raised spermidine in yeast, flies, mice **and human volunteers**. Blocking spermidine synthesis abolished fasting's lifespan benefit in animals [[12]][s12]. | Mixed | C/D |

**Does spermidine really fall with age?** The human data are mixed. In a study of whole blood, people aged 60–80 had the lowest spermidine. People aged 90 and over (nonagenarians and centenarians) had levels nearer the young group, and a higher share of spermine [[25]][s25]. That pattern fits "decline in typical aging" no better than it fits "survivors are different."

## 2. Human randomized trials

| Trial | Dose and product | Population | Duration | Primary outcome and result | Grade | Notes and conflicts of interest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SmartAge phase IIb** [[6]][s6] | **0.9 mg/day** from a spermidine-rich wheat-germ extract | **100 adults aged 60–90 with subjective memory complaints** | 12 months | **Null.** Memory (mnemonic discrimination): difference −0.03 (95% CI −0.11 to 0.05; P = .47). **Secondary outcomes were null too.** "Possible" effects on verbal memory and inflammation appeared only in exploratory analyses. | **B** (the only adequately sized, long RCT) | The best trial is null. The authors suggested higher doses next. |
| SmartAge MRI secondary analysis [[7]][s7] | 0.9 mg/day | 75 from the trial above | 12 months | Small regional changes in brain blood flow and white matter. **No change** in brain volume, cortical thickness or white-matter lesions. **The changes were not linked to memory.** | C | The authors call it exploratory and preliminary. It runs many comparisons after a null primary result. |
| SmartAge pilot (IIa) [[5]][s5] | Spermidine-rich plant extract (1.2 mg/day [[4]][s4]) | 30 adults aged 60–80 with memory complaints | 3 months | Memory improved with a "moderate" effect size (d about 0.77). The 95% CI ran **from 0 to 1.53**. | C | **The larger trial did not replicate it** [[6]][s6] |
| Safety pilot [[4]][s4] | 1.2 mg/day | Same 30 | 3 months | No safety signals | C (safety) | |

| "Fasting mimetic" blend [[26]][s26] | Spermidine plus nicotinamide plus two fatty-acid amides (PEA, OEA) | 42 overweight older adults | 8 weeks | Better hunger scores; lower LDL and glucose | C | **Sponsor-authored (Mimio Health).** A multi-ingredient product can't show what spermidine itself does. |

**Scoring on the mitochondrial review's three outcomes (Density / Efficiency / Function):** `? / ? / ?`.
- No human trial has measured muscle mitochondrial content, coupling or function after spermidine.
- The mouse heart data (better mitochondrial respiration and mitophagy) [[2]][s2] have not been tested in humans.
- In human muscle, **spermidine levels were not associated with diet or with plasma levels** [[18]][s18]. That suggests tight local regulation, which a small oral dose is unlikely to override.

## 3. The dose and absorption problem

This is the core of the problem, and marketing rarely mentions it.

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| A typical US diet supplies about **55 µmol/day of spermidine, roughly 8 mg/day** (my conversion: 54.7 µmol × 145 g/mol) | [[14]][s14] |
| Danish cardiac patients took in a median of **11.5 mg/day** from food | [[18]][s18] |
| Total dietary polyamines in UK Biobank were about **17–34 mg/day** (spermidine plus spermine plus putrescine) | [[16]][s16] |
| SmartAge's supplement gave **0.9 mg/day**, about **8–11% of normal dietary intake** (computed) | [[6]][s6] |
| **15 mg/day did not raise plasma spermidine.** It was likely converted to spermine before reaching the blood. | [[10]][s10] |
| **40 mg/day for 28 days produced no substantial change** in serum or urine polyamines | [[11]][s11] |
| Plasma spermidine rose only **18% for each doubling of dietary intake**. Muscle spermidine was **unrelated** to intake. | [[18]][s18] |

**What this implies:**
- A 1 mg capsule adds about a tenth of what your food already provides.
- The body appears to buffer even 15–40 mg/day.
- So a commercial-dose supplement is unlikely to change spermidine levels in your tissues.

The mouse studies supplied spermidine in drinking water [[2]][s2]. My rough body-weight scaling of the doses typically used in that work suggests they are tens to hundreds of times above a 1 mg human capsule. That is an estimate, not a figure from the abstracts.

## 4. Observational evidence (associations, not causation)

| Cohort | n | Finding | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bruneck, Italy** [[3]][s3] | 829, followed 20 years | All-cause death **HR 0.76 per 1-SD higher spermidine intake** (fully adjusted). Replicated in the SAPHIR cohort (HR 0.71). Top vs bottom third was "like being 5.7 years younger." | Small cohort, food-questionnaire based. Higher-spermidine diets are richer in whole grains, legumes and vegetables. Co-authored by the group that discovered the mechanism. |
| Bruneck (cardiovascular) [[2]][s2] | — | Higher dietary spermidine was associated with lower blood pressure and less cardiovascular disease | Same cohort |
| **UK Biobank** [[16]][s16] | 184,732 | **Non-linear.** The lowest mortality was in the *second* fifth of intake (HR 0.82). There was **no further benefit at higher intakes.** | This argues against "more is better." The pattern looks like a marker of overall diet quality. |
| **Takayama, Japan** [[17]][s17] | 29,079, followed 16 years | **No association** with all-cause or cause-specific mortality. A **suggestive positive link with cancer death in women** (HR 1.38, 95% CI 0.99–1.93). | A null replication in a large Asian cohort |
| **PLCO, USA** [[27]][s27] | 54,517 men | No link with prostate cancer incidence or prostate-cancer death. The inverse link with all-cause death "may reflect residual confounding" in the authors' words. | Authors explicitly warn against recommending it |
| **Women's Health Initiative** [[15]][s15] | 87,602 | Dietary polyamines were **not** positively associated with colorectal cancer. Some quintiles had lower risk. | Reassuring for intake from food |
| **Adenoma trial control arms** [[21]][s21] | 1,164 people with a prior adenoma | Above-median polyamine intake was associated with **39% higher odds of a recurrent adenoma** (OR 1.39). Odds were higher still in women (OR 2.43). | Concerning for people with a history of polyps |

**Net:** at best, the cohort data show that a polyamine-rich *diet* goes along with longer life. Whether spermidine itself causes this is unknown, and none of these studies involve supplements.

## 5. Safety and the cancer question

- **Short-term safety is reassuring.**
  - No adverse signals were seen at 0.9 mg/day for 12 months [[6]][s6], 1.2 mg/day for 3 months [[4]][s4], 6 mg/day for 13 weeks [[20]][s20], 15 mg/day for 5 days [[10]][s10], or 40 mg/day for 28 days [[11]][s11].
- **The long-term cancer question is genuinely unresolved.**
  - Polyamines are required for tumor growth. Tumors often have disrupted polyamine metabolism, and depleting polyamines is an active anticancer strategy [[13]][s13].
  - In a randomized trial, a polyamine-synthesis blocker (DFMO) plus sulindac **cut colorectal adenoma recurrence from 41.1% to 12.3%** (RR 0.30) [[21b]][s21b].
  - Food-intake studies conflict: higher adenoma risk in [[21]][s21], no colorectal-cancer risk in [[15]][s15], and a suggestive rise in cancer death in women in [[17]][s17].
  - **None of these trials ran long enough to detect a cancer signal.**
- **Who should avoid spermidine supplements** (my judgment, based on the above):
  - people with active cancer, a cancer history, or prior colorectal adenomas or polyps
  - pregnancy and breastfeeding (no data)
- **Product quality.** Most consumer products are wheat-germ extracts standardized to about 1 mg. Their polyamine content and other ingredients vary. Pure spermidine became available for human testing only recently [[11]][s11].

## 7. Hype versus evidence

| Marketing claim | What the human evidence shows |
|---|---|
| "Activates autophagy and cellular renewal" | Shown in cells and animals [[1]][s1] [[22]][s22]. No validated way to measure autophagy in living people. One pilot showed autophagy-gene signatures in B cells [[20]][s20]. |
| "Extends lifespan" | Shown in animals only [[1]][s1] [[2]][s2]. Human data are diet associations that don't agree with each other [[3]][s3] [[17]][s17]. |
| "Improves memory" | **The 12-month RCT was null** [[6]][s6]. The positive pilot was not replicated [[5]][s5]. |
| "Protects the heart" | Shown in rodents [[2]][s2]. The human trial (POLYCAD) is unpublished [[19]][s19]. |
| "Boosts immunity and vaccine response" | One 40-person pilot, positive only in non-responders [[20]][s20]. The most promising lead, but it needs replication. |
| "Fasting in a pill" | Spermidine is necessary for fasting's benefits in animals [[12]][s12]. Necessary is not the same as sufficient, and supplements don't raise blood levels [[10]][s10]. |

## 8. Practical recommendation

1. **Don't take it for longevity.** At about 1 mg/day it is very unlikely to do anything [[10]][s10] [[6]][s6]. Higher doses have no proven benefit and unresolved long-term cancer questions [[13]][s13].
2. **If you want more spermidine, eat for it.** Corn and green peas are among the richest sources in the US database [[14]][s14]. Diets that include whole grains, legumes, vegetables, mushrooms and aged cheese are the pattern linked to longevity in cohorts. Those foods are worth eating anyway.
3. **Use the levers that actually move autophagy and aging biology.** Exercise is the top lever in the mitochondrial review. Fasting raises your own spermidine [[12]][s12].
4. **Exceptions worth watching:**
   - An older adult with a documented poor vaccine response could reasonably follow the vaccine trial line [[20]][s20], ideally in a study.
   - Re-evaluate everything once POLYCAD reports [[19]][s19].
5. **Avoid spermidine supplements** if you have a cancer history or colorectal polyps [[21]][s21] [[21b]][s21b].

**What would change this verdict:**
- POLYCAD (24 mg/day) showing gains in VO₂max, lean mass or heart remodeling.
- A properly powered vaccine-response RCT.
- Any human study showing that a supplement raises tissue spermidine and an autophagy marker validated against function.
